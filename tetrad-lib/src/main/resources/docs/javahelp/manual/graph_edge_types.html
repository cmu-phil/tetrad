Understanding PAG Edge Types

A PAG (Partial Ancestral Graph) summarizes causal relationships that are consistent with the observed data when some variables may be unmeasured. Edges indicate which causal relationships are possible and which are ruled out.

Edge What This Means What Is Ruled Out
A-->B A is a cause of B. The effect may be direct or indirect (possibly through other measured variables). There may still be an unmeasured confounder affecting both A and B. B is not a cause of A.
A<->B A and B share an unmeasured common cause (a latent confounder). This confounder may influence A and B through additional measured variables. A is not a cause of B.
B is not a cause of A.
Ao->B One of the following is true:
  • A causes B
  • There is an unmeasured confounder affecting A and B
  • Both of the above
 B is not a cause of A.
Ao-oB The relationship between A and B is not determined. Exactly one of the following holds:
  1. A causes B
  2. B causes A
  3. A and B share an unmeasured confounder
  4. A causes B and there is also a confounder
  5. B causes A and there is also a confounder
 No direction of causation can yet be ruled out.

PAG Edge Specialization Markups

If PAG edge-specialization markup is enabled, directed edges may contain additional visual information:

  • Solid directed edge (A-->B)
    No latent confounder is present for this relationship. The edge is visible. In linear models its coefficient can be estimated.
  • Dashed directed edge
    There may be an unmeasured confounder affecting the relationship, so the causal coefficient may not be identifiable.
  • Thick directed edge
    The relationship is definitely direct. A directed path connecting the endpoints appears in the true underlying DAG.
  • Thin directed edge
    The relationship is possibly indirect. There may be intermediate variables along the causal pathway.
Selection Bias

The interpretations above assume there is no selection bias. Selection bias can occur when two measured variables share a common latent child rather than a common cause, which is conditioned on by selection in the observed data:

X-->(L)<--Y

In a PAG this situation is represented by:

X---Y
